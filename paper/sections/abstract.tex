\begin{abstract}
Post-training leaves a concrete record in the difference between a model's
initial and final weights. We ask whether the geometry of this checkpoint delta
can narrow the search for directions associated with refusal
\citep{ouyang2022instruct,arditi2024} and measured emergent misalignment
\citep{betley2025emergent,turner2025organisms}. We begin with spectral
visibility. Across all $224$ linear maps in the Llama-3-8B Base-to-Instruct
delta \citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, the leading eigenvalue lies above a fitted
Marchenko--Pastur edge \citep{marchenko1967}. This conclusion survives an
alternative trace-moment fit, although full-spectrum exceedance counts remain
fit-sensitive and the trace-moment top-edge check is algebraically linked to
stable rank.

Visibility alone does not attach behavioral meaning to a direction. We
therefore test the leading attention-output subspace against an independently
estimated refusal direction \citep{arditi2024}. Projecting out its top $128$
directions at every layer changes the harmful-prompt substring-refusal rate on a
fixed $128$-prompt slice from $98.4\%$ to $14.1\%$, while a same-dimensional
random projection remains at $97.7\%$. The same intervention damages MMLU,
ARC-C, and GSM8K far more than the random projection, so it is a diagnostic of
behavioral sensitivity rather than capability-preserving refusal removal.

We then use matched harmful and safe medical fine-tunes to isolate a
condition-specific weight contrast. Four Qwen checkpoint pairs yield directions
that agree with their pooled contrast (cosine $0.97$), and a retrospective
leave-one-pair-out score ranks every harmful arm above its safe counterpart. The
fit uses arm assignments but no judged responses. In an in-sample
Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct intervention \citep{hui2024qwen25coder}, projecting
out the contrast changes the joint misalignment-and-eligibility rate from
$2.3\%$ to $0.0\%$, while a random direction gives $3.4\%$. The pattern recurs
in three 7B/8B medical organisms, with a partial Mistral-7B effect
\citep{jiang2023mistral}, but the 14B intervention does not meet its frozen
positive criteria. A separate $16$-pair audit finds perfect held-out ordering
for weight-SVD, row-mean contrast, and arm-labeled activation PCA, while score
parameterization reverses the margin ordering of the two weight methods.
Together, the results support behavior-sensitive subspaces in these fixed
checkpoints, not method superiority or prospective causal generalization.
\end{abstract}
