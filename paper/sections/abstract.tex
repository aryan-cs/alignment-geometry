\begin{abstract}
Post-training changes a model's weights to produce instruction following and
refusal \citep{ouyang2022instruct,arditi2024}. We ask whether the geometry of
that change identifies directions associated with refusal and measured emergent
misalignment \citep{betley2025emergent,turner2025organisms}. Across all $224$
linear maps in the Llama-3-8B Base-to-Instruct checkpoint delta
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, the leading eigenvalue lies above a fitted
Marchenko--Pastur visibility edge \citep{marchenko1967}. All $224$ remain above
under an alternative trace-moment fit, while full-spectrum exceedance counts are
fit-sensitive. The trace-moment top-edge check is algebraically linked to stable
rank and is not independent evidence.
The empirical refusal direction \citep{arditi2024} is enriched in the leading
singular subspace of the attention-output delta. Projecting out the top-$128$
subspace at every layer changes the harmful-prompt substring-refusal rate on a
fixed $128$-prompt slice from $98.4\%$ to $14.1\%$, while a same-dimensional
random projection remains at $97.7\%$. The same projection reduces MMLU,
ARC-C, and GSM8K accuracy far more than the random projection, indicating broad
disruption rather than capability-preserving refusal removal.
For emergent misalignment, a primary four-pair Qwen medical cohort of
condition-labeled harmful and safe
checkpoints yield a matched contrast direction. The fit uses arm assignments
but no judged response examples. Four paired directions agree with their pooled
contrast (cosine $0.97$), and a retrospective leave-one-pair-out score ranks
every held-out harmful arm above its safe counterpart. In an in-sample
Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct intervention \citep{hui2024qwen25coder}, projecting
out the contrast changes the joint misalignment-and-eligibility rate from
$2.3\%$ to $0.0\%$, while a random direction gives $3.4\%$. The pattern appears
in three 7B/8B medical organisms, with a partial Mistral-7B effect
\citep{jiang2023mistral}; the 14B intervention did not meet its frozen positive
criteria. In a separate retrospective audit of $16$ suffix-matched Qwen
medical pairs, comprising the primary four plus $12$ additional pairs,
weight-SVD, row-mean contrast,
and arm-labeled activation PCA all rank every held-out pair correctly. The
relative weight-space margin of weight-SVD and row-mean reverses when the score
is squared, so the audit supports separability rather than method superiority.
Together, these fixed-checkpoint analyses identify behavior-sensitive
subspaces in the tested models; independent retraining remains untested,
leaving prospective causal generalization open.
\end{abstract}
