\begin{abstract}
Post-training leaves a record in the difference between a model's initial and
final weights. We ask whether this checkpoint geometry can narrow the search
for directions associated with refusal \citep{arditi2024} and measured emergent
misalignment \citep{betley2025emergent,turner2025organisms}. Across all $224$
linear maps in the Llama-3-8B Base-to-Instruct delta
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, every leading eigenvalue clears a fitted
Marchenko--Pastur visibility edge \citep{marchenko1967}, including under an
alternative scale fit. The spectrum therefore nominates prominent directions,
but does not identify their behavioral role.

We first test the leading attention-output subspace against an independently
estimated refusal direction \citep{arditi2024}. Projecting out its top $128$ directions changes
substring-scored refusal from $98.4\%$ to $14.1\%$, versus $97.7\%$ for a random
subspace. The same intervention causes much larger capability losses, indicating
a behavior-sensitive but entangled subspace rather than an isolated safety
switch.

We then contrast matched harmful and safe medical fine-tunes. Four Qwen
checkpoint pairs \citep{hui2024qwen25coder}
agree with their pooled direction at cosine $0.97$, rank every
omitted harmful arm above its safe counterpart, and support an in-sample
projection that changes the measured joint rate from $2.3\%$ to $0.0\%$, versus
$3.4\%$ under random ablation. The construction recurs in three 7B/8B model
families, but is weaker on Mistral \citep{jiang2023mistral} and fails the frozen
14B causal criteria. A
$16$-pair audit also finds perfect held-out ordering for several learned
contrasts, not unique superiority of weight-SVD. The evidence supports
behavior-sensitive geometry in these fixed checkpoints, not a universal
direction, method superiority, or prospective causal generalization.
\end{abstract}
